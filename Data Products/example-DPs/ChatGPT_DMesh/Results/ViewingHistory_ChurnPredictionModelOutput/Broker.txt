**Compatibility Report**

**Mismatches**

The following mismatches have been identified by both data product owners:

**Non-Negotiable Mismatches:**

1. **Data Formats:** Data Product A uses a Python dictionary, while Data Product B uses a structured format (e.g., SQL) for its output. Additionally, Data Product A's output is in SQL, while Data Product B's output is in Kafka topics.
2. **Schemas:** The schemas of the two data products differ, with Data Product A having a main data product with metadata, a data asset, and an output port, whereas Data Product B has a data asset, input ports, an output port, and control port.
3. **Distribution:** Data Product A uses a Kafka-based distribution, whereas Data Product B specifies a distribution mechanism.
4. **Data Contracts:** Data Product A has a data contract that guarantees a minimum AUC of 0.85 and requires human oversight in customer contact, whereas Data Product B's data contract specifies provider and consumer promises, SLA, and applies to the output port.

**Negotiable Mismatches:**

1. **Input Ports:** Data Product A consumes data from three input ports, while Data Product B consumes logs from internal content player systems.
2. **Control Ports:** Both data products have control ports, but their specific implementations differ (Airflow vs. Flink-based stream processing).
3. **Output Ports:** Data Product A's output port provides access to model score data via SQL, while Data Product B's output port exposes a customer viewing event stream via Kafka.

**Compatibility Analysis**

Based on the identified mismatches, the compatibility of the two data products is uncertain. The non-negotiable mismatches, such as data formats, schemas, distribution, and data contracts, need to be addressed to ensure seamless integration. The negotiable mismatches, such as input ports, control ports, and output ports, can be adapted to accommodate each other's requirements.

**Recommendations**

To resolve the mismatches, I suggest the following:

**Non-Negotiable Resolutions:**

1. **Data Format Conversion:** Develop adapters or converters to transform Data Product A's dictionary format into a format compatible with Data Product B's structured output, and vice versa.
2. **Schema Alignment:** Modify the schemas of one or both data products to ensure a common understanding of the data structure.
3. **Distribution Mechanism:** Establish a distribution mechanism that can handle the data exchange between Data Product A's Kafka-based distribution and Data Product B's unspecified distribution.
4. **Data Contract Harmonization:** Review and harmonize the data contracts of both data products to ensure they meet the requirements of both use cases.

**Negotiable Resolutions:**

1. **Input Port Adaptation:** Adapt Data Product A's input ports to accommodate Data Product B's input ports, and vice versa.
2. **Control Port Alignment:** Align the control ports of both data products to ensure seamless integration.
3. **Output Port Adaptation:** Adapt Data Product A's output port to accommodate Data Product B's output port, and vice versa.

**Additional Recommendations:**

1. Conduct a detailed analysis of the data products' technical requirements.
2. Engage in discussions with the data product stakeholders to understand their constraints.
3. Develop a proof-of-concept or pilot project to test the integration of the two data products.
4. Review and update the data contracts to reflect the new provider and consumer promises, SLA, and access management.
5. Ensure that the data mesh architecture is adjusted to accommodate the integrated data products, including updates to the catalog records, data assets, and governance requirements.

**Conclusion**

Based on the identified mismatches, the two data products are not compatible in their current state. However, by addressing the non-negotiable mismatches and implementing the suggested resolutions, the data products can be integrated, enabling the company to leverage the strengths of both data products and improve overall retention and personalization strategies.
