**Mismatches**

The mismatches identified by both data product owners are:

* Non-negotiable factors:
	+ Data formats (SQL vs. JSON)
	+ Schemas (institutional knowledge base vs. logical schema)
	+ Distributions (master list of media assets vs. single data asset containing subscription details)
* Negotiable factors:
	+ Transformation logic (nightly metadata collection vs. Spark ETL pipelines)
	+ Control ports (governance on genre taxonomies vs. access governance audit logs)
	+ Output ports (queryable metadata output port vs. snapshot table updated daily)
* Domain and ownership differences
* Value proposition differences

**Compatibility Analysis**

The compatibility analysis reveals that the mismatches are significant, and the data products are not compatible in their current form. The non-negotiable factors, such as data formats, schemas, and distributions, are fundamental to the data products and cannot be easily changed. However, the negotiable factors, such as transformation logic, control ports, and output ports, can be adapted or integrated to accommodate each other's requirements.

**Recommendations**

To resolve the mismatches, I recommend the following:
* Align the data formats and schemas
* Integrate the transformation logics to create a new transformation logic that meets the requirements of both data products
* Adapt the control ports to ensure consistent governance and access management
* Merge or integrate the output ports to create a single output port that meets the requirements of both data products
* Establish a common data format or schema that can accommodate both data products' requirements
* Harmonize the transformation logics to enable seamless data exchange and processing
* Align the control ports and governance mechanisms to ensure consistent access governance, audit logs, and policy validation across both data products

**Conclusion**

The overall compatibility of the two data products is limited due to the significant mismatches in non-negotiable factors. However, with significant adjustments and adaptations, it is possible to create a new data product that leverages the strengths of both data products, enabling better content recommendation systems, analytics capabilities, and subscription lifecycle management. If the non-negotiable mismatches are not addressed, the data products are not compatible.
