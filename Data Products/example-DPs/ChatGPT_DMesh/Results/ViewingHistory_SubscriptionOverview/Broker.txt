**Compatibility Report**

**Mismatches**

The following mismatches have been identified by both data product owners:

* **Data formats:** Kafka-based distribution (DP2) vs. snapshot table updated daily (DP3)
* **Schemas:** Logical schema for DP2 vs. logical schema defined in JSON for DP3
* **Distribution:** Real-time stream of customer viewing events (DP2) vs. daily updated table (DP3)
* **Transformation logic:** Flink-based stream processing (DP2) vs. Spark ETL pipelines (DP3)
* **Control ports:** Flink-based governance and access management (DP2) vs. IAM and policy checks (DP3)
* **Syntax:** Incompatible distribution mechanisms (Kafka-based vs. snapshot table updated daily)
* **Semantics:** Different data semantics (customer viewing event streams vs. subscription lifecycle metadata)
* **Cataloging:** Inconsistent cataloging (dependency and usage tracking)
* **Data Governance:** Different control ports for stream data governance and access governance audit logs
* **Security:** Incompatible security measures (input port security vs. subscription data protection)
* **Scalability:** Different scalability requirements (real-time stream vs. batch processing)
* **Data Quality:** Different transformation logics (Flink-based stream processing vs. Spark ETL pipelines)

**Compatibility Analysis**

Based on the identified mismatches, the compatibility of the two data products can be analyzed as follows:

* Non-negotiable mismatches: 5 (Data formats, Schemas, Distribution, Syntax, and Semantics)
* Negotiable mismatches: 7 (Transformation logic, Control ports, Cataloging, Data Governance, Security, Scalability, and Data Quality)

**Recommendations**

To resolve the mismatches, the following recommendations are suggested:

* Develop a data transformation process to convert the snapshot table of DP3 into a real-time stream for DP2's Kafka-based distribution.
* Align the logical schemas of DP2 and DP3 to enable seamless integration.
* Develop a common distribution frequency or mechanism for real-time and batch data processing.
* Adapt or modify the transformation logic of DP2 and DP3 to accommodate the integration requirements.
* Integrate the control ports of DP2 and DP3 to enable governance and access management across both data products.
* Standardize data formats and schemas across the organization.
* Develop a unified governance and access management framework that can accommodate different technologies and use cases.
* Create APIs to expose the data products, allowing them to communicate with each other while maintaining their respective architectures.
* Integrate the control ports that enforce stream data governance with those that manage access governance audit logs and policy validation for billing exposure.
* Secure the input ports that consume logs from internal content player systems to ensure that DP3's subscription data is not compromised.
* Scale DP3's real-time stream to handle the additional data from DP2.
* Integrate the transformation logic of both data products to ensure data quality and consistency.

**Conclusion**

Based on the analysis, the two data products are not compatible due to the non-negotiable mismatches.
